# 03 — Mathematical foundations, derivations, and algorithm verification

**Scope:** yaw-invariant attitude estimation from a six-axis IMU; mathematical comparison of 2024–2026 methods and the pinned TG-DCM source. **Conventions matter:** all equations below use scalar-first Hamilton quaternions and R=R_WB mapping body vectors to the world frame. They are not universal sign conventions. Source: [TG-DCM mathematics](../../docs/reference/mathematics.md), [losses.py](../../src/tg_dcm/losses.py), [so3.py](../../src/tg_dcm/so3.py).

## 1. Measurement equations and structural identifiability

Let R(t) in SO(3) be body-to-world attitude, omega in R^3 the true body angular velocity, a_W the world-frame translational acceleration at the reference point, and g_W the world gravity vector. A raw accelerometer reports **specific force**, approximately

    f_m = R^T (a_W - g_W) + b_a + n_a

and a gyro reports

    omega_m = omega + b_g + n_g.

For a displaced accelerometer with known body-frame lever arm r from the reference point, add the rotational acceleration terms in the body frame, approximately alpha x r + omega x (omega x r), plus any reference-point translational acceleration. Rotational angular acceleration alpha is a body-frame derivative of omega if the frame and lever-arm convention are held fixed. Sensor calibration matrices may additionally multiply the ideal signals. These equations assume a rigid sensor mount, correct units and synchronized timestamps.

Define e_z=(0,0,1)^T and the body-frame world-up direction

    h = R^T e_z,     ||h||=1.

If the accelerometer is near static relative to the world, bias-corrected specific force is aligned with either +h or -h depending on the sign of the chosen gravity vector and world z convention. A **dataset-specific sign test** is mandatory. The existing code assumes normalized acceleration should align with the positive h defined above; do not transfer this formula to a data source whose accelerometer reports the opposite static sign.

### 1.1 Gauge freedom: why absolute yaw is unobservable

For an arbitrary rotation about world vertical, Y=R_z(phi), define R'=YR. Since Y^T e_z=e_z,

    h' = R'^T e_z = R^T Y^T e_z = h.

The body angular velocity generated by the kinematic equation is unchanged by a **constant** left rotation Y. Moreover R'^T(a'_W-g'_W) can remain identical under the corresponding rotated world trajectory. Therefore a six-axis inertial record alone does not uniquely determine global yaw or absolute world heading. Relative angular change can be integrated, but bias creates time-growing heading uncertainty. A supervised *full* geodesic objective on an absolute GT quaternion injects an arbitrary heading reference unless initial yaw or external heading information is explicitly supplied.

The physical target of a gravity-observing six-axis attitude method is the equivalence class SO(3)/SO(2), topologically S². Its intrinsic dimension is two rather than three.

## 2. Quaternion mapping and tilt geometry

For unit wxyz Hamilton q=(w,x,y,z), the implemented DCM vertical map is

    h(q) = [2(xz-wy), 2(yz+wx), 1-2(x²+y²)].

It equals R(q)^T e_z, not the third **column** of R. This distinction must be preserved when translating C++/ROS conventions, which sometimes invert R and/or use xyzw. For predicted qhat and reference q, set

    q_err = normalize(qhat tensor conjugate(q))

and decompose q_err=(w_e,x_e,y_e,z_e). Its yaw-invariant inclination error is

    theta_tilt = 2 atan2(sqrt(x_e²+y_e²), sqrt(w_e²+z_e²)).

Equivalently, for hhat and h_gt,

    theta_tilt = atan2(||hhat x h_gt||, hhat dot h_gt).

Both formulas measure the angle between vertical directions in the same **body coordinate system**. The q_err multiplication order matters: swapping into conjugate(q) tensor qhat generally produces a different frame-referenced error. Since quaternion antipodes q and -q represent the same rotation, the formulas and model tests must be antipodally invariant.

**Finite precision:** the source [angle_between_unit](../../src/tg_dcm/losses.py) first clamps the dot product to [-1+1e-7,1-1e-7], so even exactly aligned unit vectors acquire a small positive pre-deadzone angle (~0.02562 degrees). Its dynamics and gravity deadzones (0.15 and 0.20 degrees) exceed this numerical floor, so the floor is usually removed by the positive-part residual; if this angular function is ever reused for calibration or loss without a deadzone, prefer the cross-product atan2 form or numerically stable norm-based method.

## 3. Gyro propagation on SO(3) and S²

With body angular rate omega and R mapping body to world:

    dR/dt = R [omega]_x.

Here [v]_x is the skew matrix satisfying [v]_x u=v x u. The nominal Lie-group discrete update is

    R_{k+1}^- = R_k Exp_SO3([omega_m-b_g]_x Delta t).

With quaternion delta q=Exp_q((omega_m-b_g)Delta t), TG-DCM uses q_{k+1}^-=normalize(q_k tensor delta q); Exp_q(r)=[cos(theta/2), r sin(theta/2)/theta], theta=||r||.

Because h=R^T e_z,

    dh/dt = -[omega]_x h,

so its direct propagation is

    h_{k+1}^- = Exp_SO3(-[omega_m-b_g]_x Delta t) h_k.

For a time-varying gyro, a one-sample frozen-rate exponential is a first-order approximation; integration quality depends on sampling, biases, high angular acceleration and whether angular rate is calibrated or resampled preserving increments. A learned direct quaternion output is **not automatically constrained** to this propagation at inference merely because a training loss encouraged consistency.

## 4. Exact TG-DCM objective and its conditioning

The reference implementation is [src/tg_dcm/losses.py](../../src/tg_dcm/losses.py) and configured values are in [configs/model.yaml](../../configs/model.yaml).

### 4.1 Supervised tilt

    rho_delta(x) = sqrt(x²+delta²)-delta
    L_tilt = mean over valid GT [rho_0.001(theta_tilt(qhat_t,q_t))]
    L_norm = mean_t [(||qhat_raw,t||-1)²].

The primary objective is L_sup=L_tilt+0.01 L_norm. Charbonnier is approximately quadratic x²/(2delta) near zero and approximately |x|-delta for large errors. Angles are in **radians** in code; weights must not be interpreted as degree-scale coefficients.

### 4.2 Target-gated gyro dynamics

For valid adjacent targets q_t, q_{t+1}, define

    e_gt(t) = tilt(gyro_step(q_t,omega_m,t,dt_t), q_{t+1})
    m_t = 1 if finite and e_gt(t) <= 2 degrees, else 0.

For model predictions,

    qprop_t = gyro_step(qhat_t,omega_m,t,dt_t)
    e_dyn(t) = angle(h(qprop_t),h(qhat_{t+1}))
    r_dyn(t) = max(e_dyn(t)-0.15 degrees,0)
    L_dyn = sum_t m_t rho_(0.5deg)(r_dyn(t)) / max(sum_t m_t,1).

This is a **conditional physics penalty**. Conditioning on target consistency can exclude label misalignments, but may also systematically exclude the hardest maneuvers, introducing selection bias into what the branch is actually trained to enforce. Record accepted fraction by raw motion, angular rate and GT source.

### 4.3 Confidence-gated gravity

With g=9.81 m/s², J_t an adjacent-sample normed difference divided by dt (max assigned to both neighbors), and W_t=||omega_m,t||:

    w_acc = exp[-(||f_m||-g)²/(2*0.25²)]
    w_jerk = exp[-J_t²/(2*45²)]
    w_rate = exp[-W_t²/(2*1.4²)]
    w_t = w_acc w_jerk w_rate
    s_t = 1 if w_t>0.0001 and inputs finite, else 0.

The gravity residual is e_grav=angle(h(qhat_t),f_m/||f_m||). After a 0.2-degree deadzone and Charbonnier scale 0.5 degrees, the implementation computes

    L_grav = sum_t s_t w_t rho_(0.5deg)(max(e_grav-0.2deg,0))
             / max(sum_t s_t,1).

Note **division by accepted sample count, not sum_t(s_t w_t)**. This is a confidence-scaled *branch magnitude*, not a probability-normalized expected residual. An alternative mass-normalized objective would be

    L_grav_mass = sum_t s_t w_t rho(...) / max(sum_t s_t w_t,epsilon)

but neither is automatically correct. Report gate occupancy, effective gradient scale and held-out accuracy to determine behavior. The full released objective is

    L = L_tilt + 0.01 L_norm + 0.10 L_dyn + 0.05 L_grav.

### 4.4 Temporal validity caveat

The current forward code clamps dt to at least 1e-6 **before** validating it in the gyro pair mask. Hence a finite negative or zero delta can be converted to a tiny positive integration step instead of rejected. This is a source-level correctness issue for physical consistency, not a published-model improvement. The [so3 interpolation](../../src/tg_dcm/so3.py) also forms adjacent pairs from a compressed list of valid targets, which can bridge invalid reference intervals and excludes exact final timestamps. Both need regression tests and recomputation of affected prepared data.

## 5. Robust filtering and learned covariance

For a geometric filter, let x be a nominal state with local error delta x; attitude update is R^+=R^- Exp_SO3([delta theta]_x) under a chosen right perturbation. A linearized measurement innovation is

    r = z-h(x^-),    S=H P^- H^T + R_meas,
    K=P^- H^T S^-1,  delta x=Kr.

The symbols R (rotation) and R_meas (measurement covariance) must not be confused. An adaptive acceleration filter may increase R_meas when f_m is unreliable; a mechanistic compensator can first remove known lever-arm acceleration. [Wei 2025 RAESKF](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2024.116097) is directly relevant. [Ye 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/rob.70082) instead corrects gyro signal before geometric preintegration; these methods operate at different points in the state-estimation pipeline.

For a proposed tilt innovation, choose a smooth local tangent basis B(h) in R^(3x2) with B^T h=0 and B^T B=I. A predicted local two-dimensional residual r2 and covariance Sigma2=LL^T+epsilon I define Gaussian NLL

    NLL = 0.5 (r2^T Sigma2^-1 r2 + log det Sigma2 + 2 log(2pi)).

For a calibrated two-dimensional Gaussian, E[r2^T Sigma2^-1 r2] is approximately 2 (NEES), **conditional on the covariance representing the actual error and the innovation model**. Cholesky/softplus eigenvalue safeguards prevent nonpositive-definite numerical covariance but do not guarantee statistical calibration. [EqNIO](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06321), [GINIO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25338), [KISS-IMU](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06205) and [MosaicIMU](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09355) use learned covariance in odometry settings.

## 6. Proposed S² correction geometry (hypothesis)

For a unit predicted h and tangent v with h^T v=0, the intrinsic exponential map is

    Exp_h(v) = cos(||v||) h + (sin(||v||)/||v||) v.

A network can output 2-D xi, map it through tangent basis B to v=Bxi, then compute h^+=Exp_h(v). This ensures ||h^+||=1 up to numerical error without learning a redundant yaw. For observed z in S² not antipodal to h, the logarithm is

    theta=acos(clamp(h dot z,-1,1)),
    Log_h(z) = [theta/sin(theta)] (z-cos(theta) h).

Near theta=pi, the logarithm becomes ambiguous; near zero use a stable Taylor expansion. Tangent basis discontinuities at atlas boundaries must be handled (e.g. adaptive orthonormal basis or parallel transport). A learned bias estimate and covariance update must be trained and evaluated for identifiability under excitation; otherwise the correction may absorb arbitrarily biased motions.

This S² model is **not claimed novel**: differential geometry, geometric filters, learned uncertainty and observer corrections are established ingredients. Its novelty, if any, would require a precise algorithm and proof or empirical superiority over all matched baselines.

## 7. Sensor-frame coordinate change, equivariance, and parity

Let Q in SO(3) map original body coordinates into new body coordinates. Then for body vectors v'=Qv, a correct body-to-world rotation representation changes as

    R' = R Q^T,     h'=Q h,     omega'=Q omega.

A vector-valued learned estimator should obey m(QX)=Q m(X) and a covariance should obey Sigma(QX)=Q Sigma(X) Q^T **for that specified transformation**. A bias state b'=Qb and stateful recurrent features must obey the same physically appropriate transformation, or a purported equivariant end-to-end estimator can fail despite equivariant convolution layers.

For reflections Q in O(3) with det(Q)=-1, a polar vector transforms as Qv while **angular velocity is axial** and transforms as det(Q) Q omega. [EqNIO](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06321) explicitly accounts for this under its O(2) subgroup. [GINIO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25338) explores SO(3)-equivariant modules; its public [known issues](https://github.com/chankyo123/ginio/blob/6b97f178be1a253ef44e9a05ed17432b41c00287/docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md) warn that naively treating an extra Log(R) attitude input as a vector fails the **physical remount** law R->RQ^T.

A physical remount that also changes r, temperature, sensor bias and clock may not be representable as a pure Q coordinate change; report algebraic and physical robustness separately.

## 8. A constructive non-identifiability counterexample

The current gravity gate depends only on acceleration magnitude, jerk magnitude and gyro-rate magnitude. Consider a stationary orientation, zero angular rate and a constant measured force

    f = [g sin(theta),0,g cos(theta)],    theta=30 degrees.

Its magnitude is exactly g, so the acceleration magnitude Gaussian equals 1; if f is constant, jerk=0 and gyro-rate=0, so **all three confidence factors equal 1**. Yet f points 30 degrees away from the actual up direction e_z. Such a measurement can arise from an additional constant acceleration

    a_extra = [g sin(theta),0,g(cos(theta)-1)]

under a consistent sign convention. The IMU alone cannot tell whether this is true tilt or a change in translational acceleration without further motion assumptions or information. Numerically at g=9.81, the additional acceleration is [4.905,0,-1.31429] m/s².

This counterexample is an analytical property, **not a failure-rate measurement on the released datasets**. It proves why norm/jerk/rate confidence cannot be a complete gravity-reliability test, regardless of tuning.

## 9. Static property checks and experimental obligations

An independent, small numerical verification of the geometry equations, not an execution of the TG-DCM package, confirmed approximate yaw invariance to machine precision and the 30-degree constant-magnitude counterexample. Source inspection confirms the interpolation pairing/gap and dt-clamp concerns; no package tests or checkpoint evaluations were run.

Mandatory tests before mathematical novelty claims:
- q and -q give identical h and tilt; left world yaw rotation leaves h unchanged.
- h propagated by quaternion/rotation increment agrees with Exp_SO3(-[omega]x dt) h.
- dt<=0, NaN, Inf, timestamp reversal and duplicate entries are **masked** and never converted into valid transitions by a clamp.
- No quaternion SLERP across original invalid GT rows; exact valid endpoints preserved.
- Equivariance for random Q, including output vector, body bias and covariance transformation; separate reflection parity tests.
- True stationary/hard translation cases, pure rotation with and without lever arm, noisy angular acceleration, and long bias-drift sequences.
- Compare predicted uncertainty against actual 2-D tangent residual distribution, not only its training NLL.
- Recompute released 137 standard and 422 DIODEM metrics and grouped confidence intervals after any preprocessing change.
