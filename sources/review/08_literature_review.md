# 08 · Critical literature review, 2024–2026

## Six-axis tilt estimation: information limits, bias dynamics, geometry, and experimental evidence

**Cutoff:** 13 November 2026. **Anchor:** [TG-DCM-PINN](../../README.md). **Search domain:** six-axis inertial attitude, inertial error-state filters, learned calibration, physically constrained estimation, equivariance, uncertainty and transfer. **Status:** primary paper/HTML/PDF text plus pinned source modules were inspected where accessible; publisher-only studies and third-party numerical results are flagged. No external benchmark was independently reproduced. This is a focused technical review, **not** a comprehensive systematic census of every publication.

### Abstract

Six-axis tilt estimation is an inference problem with two different ambiguities: a permanent global-yaw gauge and motion-dependent confounding between specific force and gravity. Neural architecture can exploit conditional regularity but cannot remove either ambiguity without additional assumptions. Research from 2024 through late 2026 supplies direct adaptive attitude filters, six-axis CNN–recurrent regression, explicit continuous bias dynamics, attention-based gyro correction, rigid-body motion compensation, geometrically equivariant priors, uncertainty-calibrated motion updates, and carrier-conditioned transfer. The most instructive findings concern *where the information enters*: ground-truth pose derivatives used to train a bias model, gravity alignment supplied by a filter to an equivariant network, LiDAR supplying pseudo-labels to an “IMU-only” predictor, future frames available to BiLSTM models, and temporal/initialization transformations imposed by benchmark scripts. In TG-DCM, source-level masking, interpolation and evaluation asymmetries precede any defensible claim that a more complex model is better. A well-specified observer on S² with explicit bias and bounded uncertainty is a plausible research program, not an established novel solution.

## 1. Frame the research as five questions

| Research question | What recent methods change | What they cannot establish automatically |
|---|---|---|
| What attitude variable is identifiable? | SO(3) adaptive observers; yaw-free tilt loss; S²-compatible correction | Global yaw from gravity and gyro alone |
| Why do motion disturbances break gravity feedback? | Adaptive covariance, rotational-acceleration compensation, nonminimum-phase analysis | Arbitrary translational acceleration from one specific-force vector |
| Is error due to a sensor or a motion prior? | Gyro/accelerometer debiasing, Neural ODEs, data-efficient calibration | Bias identity without initial calibration/observable excitation |
| Can geometry improve generalization? | O(2) canonicalization and SO(3) equivariant latent states | Changes in physical lever arm, bias or device mounting from coordinate symmetry alone |
| What constitutes experimental evidence? | Multi-carrier studies, self-supervised pseudo-labels, grouped statistics, source-to-checkpoint audits | Comparable tilt accuracy when training labels, sensors and future context differ |

The [mathematics chapter](03_mathematics.md) derives the actual observation/gauge model and the TG-DCM objective; [design](02_designs.md) audits evaluation semantics; [limitations](06_limitations.md) records evidence grades.

## 2. 2024: adaptive Lie correction and learned geometry

**RCAE — [Oveissi, Mirtaba and Goel, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13092).** The research objective is to estimate orientation despite noisy gyro and imperfect orientation observations. Retrospective-cost adaptive control selects a multiplicative SO(3) correction after inertial propagation. The benefit is separation between physical rotation propagation and adaptive measurement correction, instead of unconstrained additive quaternion regression. **Critical dependency:** the method observes attitude, so it is not a strict accelerometer+gyro baseline. **Transfer:** learn a bounded *tilt* innovation with a valid gravity observation and compare against a fixed ESKF under the same six-axis budget. An SO(3) correction is not itself novel.

**DIVE — [Bajwa et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2024.3370006).** The paper replaces missing or unreliable navigation feedback with neural IMU-history velocity measurements inside an on-manifold filter. Why this works: the filter propagates physically, while the network supplies a distribution-conditioned pseudo-measurement. What it does **not** prove is that long-range velocity, position or yaw is generically observable from the IMU; the predictor exploits platform motion statistics. Published localization improvements use trajectory metrics. **Transfer:** a calibrated pseudo-gravity observation/update is a more appropriate analogue than borrowing the velocity head.

**EqNIO — [Jayanth et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06321).** Neural inertial odometry is sensitive to the arbitrary horizontal orientation chosen for the same motion. EqNIO first gravity-aligns IMU data, learns an O(2)-subequivariant canonical frame, predicts displacement and covariance there and transports both back. It explicitly distinguishes polar acceleration from axial gyro under reflection (\(\omega'=\det Q\,Q\omega\)). **Behind the result:** gravity alignment is supplied by an existing filter estimate, so EqNIO does not solve the original gravity-estimation problem. Reported ATE is not tilt RMSE. **Code read:** [canonical-frame model and loss](https://github.com/RoyinaJayanth/EqNIO/tree/97b7a60a5e5cc8f0132bba343c83de114211f827/TLIO-master/src/network). **Research implication:** when moving EqNIO ideas to raw tilt, avoid using a hidden GT-aligned vertical frame as an input.

## 3. 2025: reject false gravity, model bias, and interrogate temporal context

### Adaptive filtering and angular acceleration

**RAESKF — [Wei et al., Measurement 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2024.116097).** Problem: conventional gravity correction mistakes dynamics for tilt. A nominal DCM/error-state EKF conditionally compensates motion-induced acceleration or inflates measurement covariance. Its value lies in keeping propagation, observation and uncertainty separate. The publisher summary reports substantial pitch/roll gains in selected dynamic tests; *complete tuning, dataset and methods were not independently obtained*. **Important failure mode:** a constant false-gravity vector can have \(\|f\|=g\); neither a norm threshold nor naive covariance switching can disambiguate it universally. RAESKF belongs in a directly matched six-axis comparison, unlike trajectory odometry systems.

**Rotational acceleration — [Brändle et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03681).** The contribution is more structural than a noise-rejection trick: sensor-offset angular acceleration enters the accelerometer correction loop and can produce nonminimum-phase transfer characteristics under some geometry/motion regimes. Lower feedback gain rejects disturbance but delays legitimate tilt tracking. **Implication:** evaluating only average angular RMSE misses bandwidth, phase delay and recovery. Mechanistic compensation, gain scheduling and sensor geometry are important alternatives to blanket motion rejection.

**Time/frequency analysis — [Sultan and Greiser, 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/s25165161).** A robot-arm reference permits time-domain and frequency-response comparison of classical IMU orientation methods. The chief contribution is **evaluation perspective**: phase lag and bandwidth can trade against mean error. Authors' ranking is apparatus-specific, not a universal filter theorem. A learned real-time tilt estimator should report transient delay and frequency response as well as aggregate error.

### Explicit inertial bias: the underappreciated 2025 branch

**Continuous bias dynamics — [Liu, Lin, Zhang and Ghaffari, RSS 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09495); [peer-reviewed proceedings](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss21/p009.html).** This work resolves a practical training barrier: sensor bias values are rarely annotated. Instead of regressing the bias at each sample from externally generated bias GT, the authors construct a controlled Neural ODE \(\dot b=f_\theta(b,\tilde u,\dot{\tilde u})\), integrate the corrected inertial trajectory on a Lie-group chart, then optimize *pose* GT error. Gyro bias is trained first; accelerometer bias second after gyro dynamics are frozen. Causal backward-difference cubic-Hermite splines convert discrete IMU inputs to continuous ODE controls; chart changes avoid singular local logarithm coordinates.

**What a superficial summary misses:** “no ground-truth bias labels” does not mean “no teacher.” Ground-truth pose and **finite-difference pose-derived initial bias approximations** enter training. An initial bias is assumed obtainable from static IMU calibration, and stochastic noise estimation is explicitly left outside the deterministic bias dynamics. Training/validation split the same train recordings by time (first 80%, last 20%); held-out test recordings are separate. The paper's EuRoC/TUM-VI results and real experiment cannot prove that the bias field uniquely identifies device error on arbitrary motion. **Inspected code:** [bias ODE networks](https://github.com/UMich-CURLY/Debias_IMU/blob/7eb2afcaba71d27c9c340850c19158f33c8b2f69/BiasDy/network_for_BiadDy.py), [causal spline](https://github.com/UMich-CURLY/Debias_IMU/blob/7eb2afcaba71d27c9c340850c19158f33c8b2f69/BiasDy/Interpolation.py), [Lie routines](https://github.com/UMich-CURLY/Debias_IMU/blob/7eb2afcaba71d27c9c340850c19158f33c8b2f69/BiasDy/lie_algebra.py) and [training](https://github.com/UMich-CURLY/Debias_IMU/blob/7eb2afcaba71d27c9c340850c19158f33c8b2f69/BiasDy/learning.py).

**Learned bias in invariant VIO — [Altawaitan et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06748).** A separate learned bias estimate feeds an invariant visual-inertial filter. The geometric filter retains motion structure while the neural component models calibration drift. **Limit:** visual observations and filter initialization remain part of the trajectory-estimation setup; do not transfer full-pose observability to an unaided tilt problem. **Implication:** compare explicit low-bandwidth bias state against implicit GRU memory at fixed sensor and motion budgets.

**TransIMU — [Ye et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/rob.70082).** A sliding-window transformer predicts gyro errors; the corrected gyro is integrated under an SO(3) loss. This distinguishes sensor calibration from quaternion regression but remains dependent on context and integration. The abstract's assertion of outperforming a 1.49° Gyro-Net figure with its own 1.56° angular error is numerically inconsistent if lower-is-better and the statistics are identical. This is an unresolved claim-level discrepancy, not proof that the algorithm fails.

**Bias robustness comparisons — [Park et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.46670/JSST.2025.34.2.130) and [2026 recurrent augmentation](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3691717).** These motivate a dedicated nuisance-state experiment under constant, random-walk and sensor-dependent biases. Their detailed improvement magnitudes were not independently reproduced.

### Learned motion priors and temporal sampling

**AirIO — [Qiu et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/LRA.2025.3581130).** A temporal CNN learns body-frame velocity features, with uncertainty-aware filtering and an optional attitude input. This explicitly tests how transforming inertial features before learning affects apparent observability. **Implementation caveat:** [CodeNetMotionwithRot](https://github.com/Air-IO/Air-IO/blob/7214e8a6ddee95ca07a64f01f1fa3e2f8f60df73/model/code.py) consumes attitude, and [training](https://github.com/Air-IO/Air-IO/blob/7214e8a6ddee95ca07a64f01f1fa3e2f8f60df73/train_motion.py) supplies GT rotation in that path. Do not compare that setting to a raw-six-axis predictor without declaring the additional attitude information.

**iMoT — [Nguyen, Le and Havinga, AAAI 2025](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v39i6.32664).** Progressive signal decoupling and learned motion-particle queries address temporal ambiguity in inertial velocity prediction. **IONext — [2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17089)** adds global/local motion mixing and gating. Neither publishes matched TG-style tilt error at equal causal delay. Their transferable contribution is a proposed *temporal representation experiment*, not demonstrated tilt performance.

**Tartan IMU — [Zhao et al., CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Zhao_Tartan_IMU_A_Light_Foundation_Model_for_Inertial_Positioning_in_CVPR_2025_paper.html).** Shared pretrained inertial features and platform-specific adaptation address heterogeneous motion. The [public LSTM trunk](https://github.com/superxslam/TartanIMU/blob/8a8025000f85d2c6b0692b88e15c2c91e761f7d7/tartan_imu/model/backbones/lstm.py) is runnable source, but [the public Transformer constructor](https://github.com/superxslam/TartanIMU/blob/8a8025000f85d2c6b0692b88e15c2c91e761f7d7/tartan_imu/model/backbones/transformer.py) explicitly raises NotImplementedError. **Evidence lesson:** performance claims must identify the actually released backbone.

## 4. 2026: direct six-axis competition and competing explanations of robustness

### Direct quaternion prediction under bounded future context

**[Ding, Hu, Sun and Yue, Measurement 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2026.122485).** This is the closest modern direct neural attitude comparator. The method combines axis-separated residual convolution, BiLSTM context, sample-rate encoding and a shortest-geodesic quaternion loss. A configurable window admits future samples to trade delay for smoothing. The publisher reports median attitude errors within 3° and over 10% improvement versus its chosen baselines on OxIOD/EuRoC settings. **Evidence limit:** the detailed full text, code/weights, precise split and same-metric tilt results were not independently accessible. A 2023 [six-axis CNN–BiLSTM study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2023.113105) predates it; “convolution + BiLSTM + quaternion” is not novel per se. More importantly, a BiLSTM score is not a zero-future-context streaming score unless its alignment makes that true.

### Physics-informed position/attitude learning

**[DPI-LSTM, Sensors 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26134287)** uses shared temporal features, separate position/attitude branches and kinematic consistency constraints. The strength is enforcing internal state consistency across outputs. The weakness is task dependency: initialized trajectory references and restricted flight datasets are needed to interpret position results. Its position RMSE cannot be ranked against tilt.

**[MoRPI-PINN, Scientific Reports 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-50630-y)** constrains planar inertial navigation with a specific mobile robot motion prior. Its reported large displacement improvements rely on the study's initialized and constrained motions. A kinematic penalty may reduce model error **inside that motion class** while becoming incorrect under other dynamics. “Physics-informed” is not synonymous with globally observable.

### Self-supervision and motion imbalance

**[KISS-IMU 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06205)** uses LiDAR ICP/pose-graph target generation, an IMU correction/uncertainty network and GMM-based motion balancing. Actual [GMM](https://github.com/sparolab/KISS-IMU/blob/572bfbe29e90bcff55dc1ddf90205f071851f818/src/models/gmm.py) and [confidence weighting](https://github.com/sparolab/KISS-IMU/blob/572bfbe29e90bcff55dc1ddf90205f071851f818/src/training/covweight.py) exist. **Hidden dependency:** the training teacher uses LiDAR; rare-motion weighting can magnify pseudo-label failures. The contribution to TG-DCM is a method for balancing **valid** high-dynamics motion, not evidence of unaided inertial tilt accuracy.

**[Qiu et al., 2026 — Self-Supervised Inertial Odometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25988)** uses short-window inertial preintegration teacher signals, a larger-context student and pose-graph distillation. This changes annotation dependence but can retain teacher drift. It does not construct an absolute yaw reference from self-supervision.

### Domain-conditioned experts and equivariant structure

**[MosaicIMU 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09355)** addresses cross-carrier transfer by routing a CNN latent through carrier-specialized experts; a shared decoder predicts velocity and positive variance, and a cloned-state filter updates navigation. [Prototype router](https://github.com/zoujy02/MosaicIMU/blob/e964e10b091361126d744fd3afa74e70223f42a8/src/models/moe_net.py) uses learned normalized prototypes and soft Gumbel weights; both mean/variance heads include dilated Conv1d and **BiLSTM**. [Train config](https://github.com/zoujy02/MosaicIMU/blob/e964e10b091361126d744fd3afa74e70223f42a8/configs/train_config.yaml) uses 500-sample, nominal 200-Hz windows; the actual future-delay depends on how outputs are aligned. Paper reports average ATE/RTE gains but acknowledges failures under new carriers, mounting and severe bias changes, and some carrier-specific ATE comparisons lose. Expert composition is not a robustness proof.

**[GINIO 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25338)** enforces SO(3) covariance of predicted mean and uncertainty through vector-/Lie-neuron features, including [invariant scalar gates of a vector-neuron GRU](https://github.com/chankyo123/ginio/blob/6b97f178be1a253ef44e9a05ed17432b41c00287/airio/model/vn_gru.py). **Decisive caveat:** the author's [KNOWN_ISSUES](https://github.com/chankyo123/ginio/blob/6b97f178be1a253ef44e9a05ed17432b41c00287/docs/KNOWN_ISSUES.md) reports a with-attitude input that violates physical remount covariance, mislabeled LFA, unsupported NLL/NEES table cells and mismatched training domains/checkpoints. The mathematical building block and published table evidence must be judged separately.

### Motion-acceleration compensation and sensing assumptions

**[Arzberger and Nüchter 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25784)** calibrate accelerometer/gyro intrinsic errors and lever-arm geometry, then subtract rotational acceleration terms before tilt correction. [Public C++ calibration](https://github.com/fallow24/ros_imu_calib/tree/918a19236cecd6a367921e44ac73958ab9fbb9c8) was inspected at src/calibration.cpp, src/filters.cpp and include/imu_tk/calibration.h; inherited toolkit routines should not all be attributed to new paper ideas. **Limit:** sensor-pivot geometry and angular acceleration must be known; arbitrary translation remains confounded.

**[ALAC 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06308)** calibrates a triaxial accelerometer efficiently with known attitude information; it is a calibration procedure, not online tilt from arbitrary unaided motion. **[Tchonkeu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13254)** derive nonlinear attitude observers using barometric and optional magnetic observations; convergence claims are conditional on those modalities. **[AI-IO 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00597)** uses quadrotor rotor-speed information; its added physical observables explain gains not guaranteed by raw six axes. **[Zhai et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2025.119824)** combine an additional angular accelerometer with gyro and learned noise covariance; also not same sensor input.

### An uncomfortable empirical null result

**[A Large-Scale Empirical Study of Deep Learning for MEMS Gyroscope Bias Estimation, 2026](https://doi.org/10.31224/7144), preprint.** Authors report **2,811 runs** across MLP/LSTM/TCN/Transformer on EuRoC and TUM-VI, with LOSO and multiple seeds. In their variance decomposition, *dataset* explains approximately 97% of differences and *model family* under 1%. A tested SO(3) regularizer yields no improvement; removing a label-denoising step changes method rankings. The paper states that EuRoC bias supervision comes from batch optimization and TUM-VI angular-rate targets derive from differentiated, smoothed pose. **Interpretation:** these are strongly different label-generation mechanisms. The quantitative 97% finding applies only to this two-dataset bias setting and is not a theorem about all inertial ML. Its real lesson is that **label construction can dominate an architectural ablation**. The work is not peer-reviewed and its experiments were not independently replicated.

## 5. TG-DCM: the code-level result behind the paper title

Actual source [losses.py](../../src/tg_dcm/losses.py) combines valid-target yaw-invariant tilt Charbonnier, output quaternion norm penalty, target gyro-consistency gating and input-derived gravity confidence from \(\|f\|-g\), jerk and \(\|\omega\|\). The gyro gate retains GT transitions with propagated tilt discrepancy at most 2°, then penalizes predicted vertical propagation over a 0.15° deadzone; the gravity loss uses 0.20° deadzone and a product of three Gaussian confidence factors. The model at [train_impl.py](../../src/tg_dcm/train_impl.py) remains a two-layer GRU with 200 hidden units and no runtime physics correction or calibrated covariance.

**What it achieves:** the released standard 137-sequence mean tilt RMSE is 1.521°, with a paired model-minus-RIANN mean -0.091° (95% CI [-0.147,-0.036]). **What remains unresolved:** the effect of each auxiliary term, seed-to-seed variation, DIODEM generalization (422 segments from 86 raw motions; p95 44.20°, max 62.38° and CI versus RIANN crossing zero), and exact runtime trust.

**Previously hidden implementation constraints:**

1. [so3.py](../../src/tg_dcm/so3.py) interpolates over compressed valid GT rows, so reference gaps can become artificial SLERP labels; exact final endpoints may be excluded.
2. [losses.py](../../src/tg_dcm/losses.py) clamps dt before physical validity and applies mask multiplication after residual calculation, allowing NaN contamination by \(0\times\mathrm{NaN}\). Zero-norm finite GT also passes its finite-target test.
3. [evaluate.py](../../scripts/evaluate.py) retrospectively overwrites early VQF outputs using a later output and evaluates RIANN/VQF using fixed dt versus TG per-frame dt.
4. The function named align_heading applies a **full** GT-derived SO(3) orientation correction to secondary roll/pitch, not only yaw; the primary unaligned tilt metric is separate.
5. Entirely absent GT can be converted to an all-true identity comparison; a norm-only “static” gate does not prove rest.

These are source-level hazards. Whether any particular dataset triggers them and how the headline numbers change require a reproducible raw-data replay. **Do not turn the audit into an unsupported assertion that all published results are wrong.**

## 6. Cross-study conclusions, distinguished by evidence strength

| Claim | Evidence strength | Defensible inference |
|---|---|---|
| Six-axis global yaw gauge and gravity/translation aliasing | Mathematical observation model | Absolute heading and arbitrary dynamic gravity separation are not generally identifiable |
| Gyro bias merits an explicit, slow-time-scale model | RSS 2025 paper + released code; 2026 preprint | A bias head/ODE is worth a controlled test, but needs initialization/excitation and noise accounting |
| Rigid-body lever-arm compensation can retain gravity information | Brändle analysis + Arzberger–Nüchter method | Benefit conditional on calibrated geometry and angular-rate derivative quality |
| SO(3)/O(2) equivariance can reduce coordinate-frame redundancy | EqNIO/GINIO theory + source modules | Guarantees apply to precisely defined *complete* input/state transformations |
| Carrier-specialized architectures generalize better on every device | Not established; MosaicIMU admits counterexamples | Must evaluate held-out devices and mounting, not just averaged ATE |
| Physics penalty always helps bias/tilt learning | Not established; 2026 bias preprint reports a null SO(3) regularizer | Need factorial, seeded ablations and label-quality controls |
| TG-DCM gate caused the standard aggregate gain | Not established by released single-run evidence | Reproduce protocol and component ablations before causal attribution |
| BiLSTM/Transformer is inherently more accurate in live tilt | Not established with matched delay | Compare at equal future lookahead, data, computation and valid truth |

## 7. Research direction with the highest information gain

The next experiment should be **diagnostic**, not a large new model: reproduce raw baseline predictions under strictly equal startup and timestamp policies; fix GT masks and NaN-safe losses; measure error against motion severity, device and label reliability. These experiments distinguish data artifacts, motion ambiguity and genuinely underfit dynamics.

Then compare a causal **gyro-bias-conditioned S² tilt observer with bounded 2×2 uncertainty** against a same-size GRU and a modern six-axis ESKF. Optionally compensate rotational acceleration under *known* lever-arm conditions and test exact algebraic coordinate equivariance separately from physical remount OOD. This combines already known ingredients; a scientific novelty claim would need a precisely new inference rule or guarantee, and reproducible tail improvement beyond those established baselines.

**Reject a proposed result** if its gain disappears when measurement channels, future samples, startup, calibration, reference validity, seed distribution or independent raw-motion grouping are equalized. A strong negative result that isolates a false assumption is more useful than an unsupported best-score label.

## Second-pass correction: an evidence result, not another network family

The primary literature review initially omitted the **2025 DIODEM data descriptor** despite DIODEM contributing 422 of 559 evaluated segment files. [Bachhuber et al., Scientific Data 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-05468-w) explain that the published benchmark contains ten rigid/foam-mounted IMUs across articulated segments, optical markers with body-to-earth orientations, explicit sensor-to-segment rotation and earth/optical coordinate alignment, and 11 continuous experiments subdivided into motion types. The spectrum of foam-attachment artifacts is broad. As a result, treating its 86 recorded motions as 86 independent acquisition sessions, or explaining high error entirely as network distribution shift, omits the defining experimental design.

A second omission was **[Daniel Weber's modern RIANN training reimplementation](https://github.com/daniel-om-weber/riann_dev/tree/435274141c08ab318c01be904de9754943b4feed)**. It shares the general GRU/augmentation/multi-rate validation design and has trainable source. It is distinct from original lost RIANN training code and distinct from the official frozen ONNX model. Its EuRoC preparer also subtracts reference-estimated gyroscope bias. The right novelty comparator is a *trained* matched RIANN-style GRU with and without target-gated physics, not architecture reputation.

**New 2025–2026 geometric prior art:** [Bouazza et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21163) use optical flow and a Riccati observer to jointly recover velocity and body gravity, then attitude; [Abi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26612) use a reduced-order body-gravity/velocity observer with visual bearing derivatives and bias augmentation; [Boughellaba et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07156) use an extended nonlinear visual–inertial Lie-group observer. Their vision-based sensing permits convergence guarantees unavailable to arbitrary six-axis motion, but confirms that explicit gravity-vector and geometric observer states are well-established prior art.

**Corrected TG-DCM empirical interpretation:** the 137 standard files contain 18 duplicated Caruso sensor streams. The original paired TG-minus-RIANN mean of -0.091 degrees has CI excluding zero, but omitting duplicate orig variants gives -0.043945 degrees with approximate CI [-0.1004,+0.0105], crossing zero. Six EuRoC test recordings and 71 OxIOD recordings have *worse* TG mean than RIANN; EuRoC gyro is reference-debiased. DIODEM has 11 higher-level sessions, and its raw-motion or session-level grouped paired intervals include zero. Therefore aggregate evidence does **not** establish robust all-domain improvement or causal component novelty before protocol repair. All figures and source routes are documented in [second-pass verification](09_second_pass_verification.md).

**Revised synthesis:** The largest information gain in this research program comes first from reconciling duplicated raw streams and reference-assisted preprocessing, then from error attribution under validated experiments. Any further S²/bias/covariance proposal remains a *hypothesis* subject to those controls.
