# Self-Supervised Inertial Odometry

**Reference:** Yuheng Qiu; Yutian Chen; Carlton Cort; Weison Huang; Shibo Zhao; Pieter Abbeel; Rocky Duan; Koushil Sreenath; C. Karen Liu; Carmelo Sferrazza; Junyi Geng; Sebastian Scherer; Guanya Shi. 2026 arXiv preprint, 2026. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25988. arXiv: 2610.25988.

## Problem and motivation

Each new robot embodiment needs expensive ground-truth trajectory labels before learning a motion prior.

## Architecture / algorithm / rationale

Integration self-supervision: a myopic IMU preintegration teacher creates short-window velocity-change targets, a context-rich student denoises them, and pose-graph distillation then produces sequence-consistent targets.

## Mathematical or identifiability mechanism

Teacher Delta v over a short window is obtained by IMU preintegration; student trained on many windows can average teacher noise under recurrent motion priors, then optimize with graph-consistent pseudo-labels.

## Experimental evidence actually verified

Authors evaluate six datasets and report average velocity error within 0.07 m/s of matched supervised baselines; no dedicated tilt RMSE evidence.

## Limitations and critical interpretation

The teacher may inherit gyro/acceleration bias; student gains depend on repeated embodiment dynamics and sufficient excitation. Self-supervision alone cannot create an absolute yaw reference.

## Actionable connection to TG-DCM-PINN

Try physics-generated tilt consistency pseudo-targets only with explicit identifiability analysis and controlled bias/linear-acceleration tests.
