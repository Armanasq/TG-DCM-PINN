# AI-IO: An Aerodynamics-Inspired Real-Time Inertial Odometry for Quadrotors

**Reference:** Jiahao Cui; Feng Yu; Linzuo Zhang; Yu Hu; Danping Zou. 2026 preprint/conference, 2026. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00597. arXiv: 2603.00597. 
**Applicability:** Transferable inertial estimation. **Evidence accessed:** primary_full_text_sections_plus_code. **Code inspected:** SJTU-ViSYS-team/AI-IO.
**Review cutoff:** 2026-11-13. **Independent replication:** no.

## Problem and motivation

For quadrotors, body velocity may be poorly identified from accelerometer and gyro history without modeling aerodynamic drag and thrust.

## Architecture / algorithm / rationale

Combine inertial sensors with rotor speed to make drag/thrust more informative, use CNN features + transformer encoder to predict velocity/uncertainty, fuse with uncertainty-aware EKF.

## Mathematical or identifiability mechanism

Authors model a_x approximately -lambda_x omega_m^2 v_x - k1 v_x - k2 v_x^2 + bias + noise; using omega_m (motor speed) changes input observability relative to six-axis IMU.

## Experimental evidence actually verified

Authors report 36.9% gain from rotor speed and 22.4% additional gain from architecture in velocity prediction; inspected actual IMUTransformerWithModality and heteroscedastic Gaussian loss.

## Limitations and critical interpretation

Not IMU-only in the strict six-axis setting; windless or simplified aerodynamic assumptions and platform-specific rotor information limit transfer.

## Actionable connection to TG-DCM-PINN

Use as justification for declaring observability assumptions and testing conditional side-channel sensing, not as a strict six-axis tilt baseline.
