# Learning-based Attitude Estimation with Noisy Measurements and Unknown Gyro Bias

**Reference:** Parham Oveissi; Mohammad Mirtaba; Ankit Goel. arXiv preprint, 2024. DOI: https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2401.13092. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13092. arXiv: 2401.13092. 
**Applicability:** Attitude estimation with a different observation model. **Evidence accessed:** primary_full_text_sections. **Code inspected:** none verified.
**Review cutoff:** 2026-11-13. **Independent replication:** no.

## Problem and motivation

Gyroscope integration drifts under unknown bias; an SO(3) filter must correct the rotation without breaking its manifold structure.

## Architecture / algorithm / rationale

Retrospective Cost Attitude Estimator (RCAE): propagate rotation with the measured angular increment and apply a multiplicative correction tuned by retrospective-cost adaptive control. The correction avoids the MEKF measurement Jacobian and does not explicitly estimate gyro bias.

## Mathematical or identifiability mechanism

R_{k+1} = R_k exp([omega_m]x Delta t) exp([u_k]x), with the second exponential a data-adaptive correction; note the paper's measured orientation input rather than reducing it to a gravity-only observation.

## Experimental evidence actually verified

Numerical simulations and physical attitude-estimation comparisons with a multiplicative EKF are described in the primary paper. No matched TG-DCM dataset or tilt metric is published.

## Limitations and critical interpretation

Crucial task mismatch: the estimator is provided noisy orientation measurements, not only a six-axis acceleration/gyro stream; do not call it a six-axis-only competitive baseline without replacing that measurement channel.

## Actionable connection to TG-DCM-PINN

Consider multiplicative/SO(3) correction or retrospective adaptation as a separately benchmarked filter head; preserve the gravity-only yaw gauge.
